01 / THE DECISION
One product. One shared scientific engine.
Three ways to use it.
This plan consolidates the earlier TrendSleuth and ClimaClue concepts, the mentor build-guide direction shared by
the team, the selected NASA challenge and the Bangladesh Participants Guide. The goal is not to build a generic
climate dashboard or a collection of disconnected demos.
BhuSanket is a question-first investigation platform. A user chooses a place and asks whether a measurable
Earth-system variable is changing. The platform retrieves a compatible NASA dataset, creates a time series,
performs a reproducible statistical test and explains the result with its uncertainty and source history.
The central user journey
ASK a real
 question
LOCATE a place
TRACE NASA data
TEST the trend
UNDERSTAND the
 verdict
The product’s five defining principles
• Question-first: begin with “Is rainfall changing here?” rather than a technical parameter catalogue.
• Evidence-first: every chart and verdict is tied to a named NASA product, variable, units, dates and processing
steps.
• Honest uncertainty: “no significant trend” and “not enough evidence” are valid outcomes, not failures.
• Learning by doing: students and public users learn to test claims rather than simply consume charts.
• Bangladesh-first, not Bangladesh-only: start with a national case library; keep the data workflow
location-independent.
THE X-FACTOR
Claim Court turns the same scientific engine into a short evidence-investigation game. The Analysis-Window Slider lets
users see whether a claim depends on a cherry-picked time period. A regional comparison reveals that one variable can
trend differently in different places.
What success looks like
A first-time student can complete one real investigation without help; a researcher can reproduce the result from
its Data Passport; and a judge can understand the product’s value in under ten seconds and see the working
evidence trail in the live demo.
Important: this strategy improves the quality of the entry but cannot guarantee a local award, global nomination or Global Award.

Distinctive visual motifs
• Evidence-case labels: numbered case IDs, compact source tags and “case closed” stamps.
• Contour-line / orbital mark: a subtle brand motif connecting Earth mapping and satellite observation.
• Mission-to-evidence trail: a visual path from satellite/product to variable, processing, test and verdict.
• Real data as visual material: maps, time-series traces, uncertainty bands and missing-data markers — not
random stock space imagery.
• Purposeful motion: slider recomputes results, map selection updates the chart, and the verdict stamp appears
only after analysis completes.
Interaction rules
• One obvious primary action per screen.
• Use plain labels such as “Choose a place” and “Test this claim”; keep advanced settings in a drawer.
• Do not rely on colour alone: every verdict has a word, icon and explanatory text.
• Use readable chart labels, keyboard-friendly controls, mobile-responsive layout and accessible contrast.

TWO DIFFERENT “NO” STATES
“No significant trend detected” means the test did not find sufficient evidence of a monotonic trend in the selected record.
“Not enough evidence” means coverage, record length, data quality or unresolved assumptions prevent a defensible
verdict. These must never be merged into one vague status.
Do not say that a place is “safe”, “unsafe”, “doomed”, “flood-prone next year” or “caused by climate change”
based on a trend test alone. Trend detection is not forecasting or causal attribution.

Claim Court is not a separate toy. It is a playable version of the same scientific investigation engine used by

Evidence tool
What it reveals
Full record
The complete available time series, not just the short interval quoted in a claim.
Season check
Like-for-like seasonal totals or a validated seasonal treatment.
Trend test
Slope, p-value, 95% confidence interval and quality warnings.
Neighbour check
The same variable in a second location to test whether the pattern is regional.
Verdict choices
 Stamp
Meaning
SUPPORTED
The full-record analysis supports the claimed direction under the defined variable, location
and period.
WINDOW-DEPENDENT
A selected sub-period tells a materially different story from the full record.
NOT SUPPORTED ON THIS RECORD
The full record does not support the claimed direction.
NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE
The record is too short, gappy, low quality or blocked by unresolved statistical
assumptions.
Scoring rules
Suggested game scoring: +3 for a correct final verdict; +1 for each unspent evidence token; at least one tool must
be used; −2 for confidently stamping a misleading claim as SUPPORTED. Add a confidence slider: a confident
wrong answer should cost more than an appropriately cautious answer. Keep the score explanation visible.
Pilot cases
• Dhaka summer temperature claim — test a defined summer air-temperature metric and full record.
• Rajshahi annual rainfall claim — define “drier” explicitly as a change in annual rainfall total; do not imply drought
without a separate definition.
• Khulna monsoon rainfall claim — compare like-for-like monsoon totals.
• Add Sylhet/Sunamganj as a case only after verifying data availability and choosing a defensible variable.
CASE SELECTION RULE
These are candidate questions, not pre-announced findings. Run the analysis first, then choose cases that provide a
balanced mix of supported, window-dependent, unsupported and insufficient-evidence outcomes. Never write the verdict in
advance.

09 / RESEARCH WORKSPACE
Give researchers control without
overwhelming everyone else.
Research Workspace is the advanced path, not the default landing page. It should make analysis reproducible and
transparent rather than adding complexity to the public experience.
Control
Expected behaviour
Variable catalogue
Searchable variables grouped by land, atmosphere, water and vegetation; each includes unit,
source and meaning.
Dataset picker
Only show products compatible with the variable, period, region and selected temporal
aggregation.
Geography
Point, grid cell or polygon selection; show the actual spatial support and valid-pixel coverage.
Time range
Display the actual available dates; warn about short records and gaps.
Aggregation
Annual, calendar month, like-for-like season or scientifically appropriate seasonal method.
Quality controls
Allow inspection of QC flags, fill values, missingness and excluded observations.
Statistical settings
Mann–Kendall variant, autocorrelation handling, confidence level and multiple-testing adjustment
where applicable.
Comparison
Compare the same variable and compatible product across locations; do not compare unlike
measurements.
Export
CSV time series, JSON result, chart image and a reproducibility report / PDF.
Research output package
• Raw or source-linked data reference and the processed time series.
• A Data Passport with collection version, parameter, units, period and spatial context.
• Aggregation and quality-control decisions.
• Statistical test, slope, confidence interval, p-value and assumptions.
• Code / app version and retrieval timestamp.
• A short interpretation that distinguishes result from hypothesis or causal explanation.
REPRODUCIBILITY TEST
A researcher should be able to take the exported Data Passport and processed series, repeat the same method and recover
the same numerical result within the documented tolerance.

14 / SCIENTIFIC ENGINE
Numbers must be defensible — and
repeatable.
The statistical engine is deterministic Python code. It receives a prepared time series and returns a structured
result. An AI model may explain that result, but it must never calculate or invent the statistics.
RAW DATA
QUALITY CHECK
AGGREGATE
MANN–KENDALL
THEIL–SEN + CI
VERDICT
Stage
Implementation rule
1. Ingest
Preserve source file / API response, product version, retrieval timestamp, units and original metadata.
2. Clean
Parse time, apply scale factors and fill values, inspect quality flags, remove invalid observations and record
gaps. Never silently interpolate.
3. Aggregate
Temperature: annual mean or mean of daily maxima, explicitly defined. Rainfall: annual or monsoon total.
Use like-for-like seasons.
4. Trend test
Mann–Kendall for monotonic trend; use a seasonal method or defensible seasonal aggregation when
seasonality matters.
5. Slope
Theil–Sen slope; report change per year or decade in original units and a 95% confidence interval.
6. Dependence
Inspect serial autocorrelation. If material, apply a validated correction (e.g., Hamed–Rao) or withhold the
significance verdict.
7. Multiple testing
When scanning many districts / variables, control false discoveries (e.g., Benjamini–Hochberg FDR) and label
adjusted results.
8. Validate
Unit-test rising, falling, flat, seasonal, tied, missing and autocorrelated synthetic series; compare outputs
with a trusted implementation.
Result states
 State
Meaning shown to user
Significant increase / decrease
Evidence supports the direction; show slope, p-value and CI.
No significant trend detected
The test did not find sufficient evidence of a monotonic trend. This does not prove that
nothing changed.
Not enough evidence
Coverage, record length, data quality or unresolved assumptions prevent a reliable
verdict.
Window-sensitive
The selected interval and full record lead to materially different conclusions.
STATISTICAL SAFETY
Statistical significance is not the same as practical importance. A small change can be significant; a large-looking change
can be uncertain. Show magnitude and uncertainty together. Do not claim causation or forecast future conditions from a
trend test.
